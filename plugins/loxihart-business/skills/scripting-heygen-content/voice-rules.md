# Voice Rules — Full Profile

Kyle Rosebrook / Abstrekkt brand voice. Every script must conform. Apply before drafting, scan before outputting.

---

## DO

- First person, singular ("I", "my", "me") — never "we" unless referring to Kyle + client working together on a project
- Short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Earned authority: state what you did, not what you know in the abstract
- Specific over general: "ten years in hospitality" not "years of experience"
- Acknowledge the audience's existing competence before positioning anything
- End with a soft close — one sentence invitation, no hard sell

---

## DO NOT

- Hype phrases (see banned list below)
- Rhetorical questions as openers ("Have you ever wondered...?")
- Passive voice ("mistakes were made", "this can be seen as...")
- Reference Loxihart or any employer context
- Co-founder framing — Kyle is solo founder under Abstrekkt
- ChaosClubCo references — retired brand, absorbed by Abstrekkt June 2026
- Reference CCA-F exam directly — encode as "Anthropic-certified" only if referenced, and only after July 20, 2026

---

## Banned Phrase List

Scan every script for these before outputting. Any hit requires revision.

**Hype / motivational:**
- "unlock" (in any motivational context)
- "transform your" (anything following)
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "leverage" (business context)
- "empower" / "empowering"
- "journey"
- "impact" (used as a verb)
- "passion for"
- "excited to share"
- "at the end of the day"

**Opener phrases:**
- "Have you ever wondered"
- "What if I told you"
- "Today, I want to talk about"
- "In today's fast-paced world"

**Vague experience framing:**
- "years of experience" (without a number)
- "in my career" (too general)
- "I've always believed"

## Voice Anchor Sentences

Use these to calibrate tone before and during drafting. If a sentence feels distant from these, revise.

- "I spent ten years in hospitality before I taught myself everything I know about AI."
- "You don't have to be technical to use AI at the level that changes your work."
- "No hype. No gatekeeping. Just the work."
- "Most companies are automating the wrong things."

---

## Audience Types

### Domain Expert
Someone skilled at their job who encounters AI as a new tool layer. Lead with respect for their existing competence — they don't need AI explained to them as magic. They need to know what it can do at their specific skill level.

**Framing pattern:** "You already know X. AI changes how fast you can reach Y."

### Curious Learner
Someone exploring AI for work, not as a technical practitioner. Lead with permission — they don't need a CS degree, they need a handle. Avoid jargon without context.

**Framing pattern:** "You don't have to be technical. You have to be willing to try."

### Default: Same Script Serves Both
Unless user specifies otherwise, write for the curious learner without condescending to the expert. Curious learners need the permission to try; experts will filter out what they already know and keep the rest.
