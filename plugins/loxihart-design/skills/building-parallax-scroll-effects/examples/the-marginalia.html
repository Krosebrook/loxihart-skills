Issue No. 14 — On Returning

Essays on the slow second read.

A quarterly for writing that asks to be read twice — once for what it says, once for what it withholds.

A Note on Attention
Words hold their shape only if you leave room around them.
— From the editor's note, Issue No. 14

Most writing asks to be consumed once. This issue doesn't. Every piece in it survived a rule we set two years ago and have broken exactly never: nothing runs unless it changes on a second read.

That's a higher bar than "well-written." A well-written paragraph can still spend its whole meaning on the first pass. What we're after is the opposite — a line that means one thing at ten in the morning and something quieter at midnight, without a single word changing.

We don't think that's a taste preference. We think it's the difference between writing that fills time and writing that survives being reread on a bad week, a good week, five years from now.

Fourteen issues in, the rule has cost us more drafts than it's kept. That's the point of having it.
