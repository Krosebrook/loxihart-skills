# Psychology Notes — Fart Prompt

Supporting context for the mechanism behind the fart prompt technique.
Not required at runtime — read when coaching users who want to understand
*why* this works, or when adapting the technique for edge cases.

---

## Activation Energy

In chemistry, activation energy is the minimum energy required to start
a reaction. In creative and productivity contexts, it's the psychological
barrier to beginning a task.

High-stakes tasks raise activation energy because the brain frames
"starting" as high-risk: if the outcome matters, starting exposes you
to failure. The loop:

```
Task feels important
  → Starting = risk of failure
  → Brain avoids starting
  → Avoidance feels safer than a bad output
  → Paralysis
```

The fart prompt collapses this loop by *reframing the goal*. The target
is now "produce something deliberately bad" — which is trivially achievable.
You cannot fail at producing a terrible output. Activation energy drops to
near zero because failure is impossible by definition.

This is a behavioral intervention, not a motivational one. It doesn't
require you to feel differently about the task. It changes the task.

---

## Perfectionism Loops

Perfectionism-induced procrastination is distinct from laziness or
disorganization. The mechanism:

1. **High internal standard** — the person genuinely cares about quality
2. **All-or-nothing framing** — anything below that standard feels like
   failure, not progress
3. **Pre-emptive avoidance** — don't start, don't fail
4. **Identity protection** — "I haven't written it yet" preserves the
   possibility that it could be great; a bad draft closes that door

The fart prompt interrupts at step 2. By making "bad" the target, the
all-or-nothing frame collapses. A bad draft is a *successful* fart prompt
output. Nothing is foreclosed — you can still produce the good version
after.

**What this is NOT:** It is not a cure for perfectionism as a trait.
It is a session-level interrupt, not a long-term identity shift.

---

## Why Structure Is the Enemy Here

Most productivity interventions add structure: frameworks, templates,
plans, question sets. The fart prompt explicitly removes structure.

The reason: for a blocked person, structure is another form of the
problem. "Write a five-paragraph essay structure" raises the stakes.
"Write whatever comes out, badly" does not.

Corollary: when applying this skill, resist the impulse to provide
frameworks, organize the output, or editorialize. The mess *is* the
product. Tidying the mess before the person asks for it defeats the
mechanism.

---

## Connection to Construction over Recovery

Kyle's core operating philosophy: build new infrastructure rather than
restoring what was lost. Don't wait for conditions to be right — start
building.

Applied to creative blocks: the fart prompt is a Construction move.
Instead of waiting to recover the perfect version of an idea (which may
never come), you construct something imperfect immediately. Momentum
is the asset. The ugly draft is a construction, not a failure.

The fart prompt is not recovery work. It does not try to repair the
blocked state. It builds past it.

---

## When This Does NOT Apply

- **Block caused by missing information** — if you're stuck because you
  don't have the facts, a fart prompt generates noise, not momentum.
  Prescribe research first.
- **Block caused by external dependency** — waiting on someone else.
  Fart prompt generates content no one will use yet.
- **Engineering architecture blocks** — where "intentionally bad" can
  normalize genuinely unsafe patterns. Use sparc-methodology-coach instead.
- **Emotionally loaded memoir content** — proceed with extra care.
  The technique works, but the mess may surface difficult material quickly.
  Validate that the person is in the right state before running it.
